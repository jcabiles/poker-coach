## MOST LIKELY MISSED

- Finnish next-action-prediction thesis — unusually direct human-action-prediction work in Hold’em, and non-English.
- PHH dataset — its README identifies 21.6m anonymized human NLHE hands and exact network counts; provenance/legal status needs separate diligence.
- *VEXBOT WINS POKER TOURNAMENT* — small historical publication, easy to miss behind the better-known Polaris narrative.
- Alberta’s two opponent-modelling dissertations — more operationally relevant than solver papers for a believable adaptive bot.
- MCRNR — bounded exploitation offers a technical middle ground between static dials and pure best response.
- Game-log supervised-learning poker paper — closest located explicit behaviour-cloning formulation.
- Maia — strongest transferable precedent for “human-like” as conditional next-action prediction, not playing strength.
- *Learning Models of Individual Behavior in Chess* — supports per-player latent/style conditioning rather than a single population bot.
- Human-trace playback paper — explicitly values believable imperfections, a useful antidote to solver-only objectives.
- BotPrize — a directly comparable blind “is this human?” north-star protocol.
- PHH’s inclusion of Pluribus hands — exposes that milestone release material is primarily agent records, not a usable human corpus.
- UCI Poker Hand — important negative finding; avoid accidentally training/evaluating on a synthetic classification set.
