# Lane A — Academic Evidence: Human-Likeness in Poker & Game AI

Sealed research lane. No knowledge of other lanes. All citations retrieved this session via WebSearch/WebFetch; verbatim URLs and `as of` dates recorded. Grades: **A** = retrieved primary source, **B** = retrieved credible secondary source, **C** = single source/weak provenance, **D** = unverified recall (listed separately, not used as evidence).

---

## Bottom line (5 bullets)

1. **The academic poker literature (University of Alberta CPRG, 1997–2017) is almost entirely about beating humans or exploiting a modelled opponent's weaknesses — not one retrieved source targets making a bot *feel* human.** Opponent modelling (Loki/Poki, Vexbot, Restricted Nash Response, Bayesian opponent exploitation) explicitly optimizes exploitation or robustness, and the one source that states this outright (Johanson/Zinkevich/Bowling, NIPS 2007) does so in its own words. Grade A/B, no contrary source found.
2. **The general game-AI field DOES have a rigorous, separate literature on human-likeness as its own objective**, with concrete, reusable measurement recipes: judge/discriminator Turing tests (BotPrize, "Navigates Like Me"), move-prediction accuracy/likelihood (Maia in chess), and behavior-cloning-from-logs pipelines (Drivatar, CS:GO). This is the literature that actually answers the project's question, not the poker-strength literature. Grade A/B across the board.
3. **Small, fast learned models are the literature-supported way to hit both human-likeness and a speed budget.** Maia (chess move-prediction net), CS:GO behavioral cloning (deep net, required to "train and run in real time"), and PCSP (persona-conditioned RL policy, 22x faster than an LLM policy, sub-frame in Unreal Engine 5) are all learned, all small enough for real-time/low-latency inference — none are LLM-per-decision. Grade A.
4. **Where measured, hand-authored/rule-based human-likeness plateaus below human baselines, and reaching or exceeding the baseline required learning from real human data, not more rule tuning.** BotPrize 2012 winners (UT^2, MirrorBot) scored above humans only after using neuroevolution/behavioral adaptation, and even the *human* players in that same tournament rated only ~41% humanness — the ceiling itself is noisy and not high. "Navigates Like Me" (CHI 2023) passed a Turing test only with an agent explicitly trained toward human movement statistics, not a baseline heuristic agent. Grade A/B.
5. **Poker-specific "imitate a style" work exists but is thin, older, and mostly about classification/clustering for exploitation, not generation of humanlike play.** Teófilo & Reis (arXiv 2013) cluster real players into ~7 style archetypes from move-frequency data — a population-modelling precedent directly relevant to this project's dial architecture — but the goal stated in the abstract is improving an opponent-modelling agent's predictions, not producing a bot other humans would rate as human. Grade A (abstract retrieved), no primary poker source found that optimizes for a human-rated humanness score.

## Thread 1 — University of Alberta CPRG lineage

**Lineage confirmed:** Loki (1997) → Poki (1999) → PsOpti/Sparbot (2002) → Vexbot (2003) → Hyperborean (2006) → Polaris (2007, 2008) → Hyperborean Ring (2009) → Cepheus (2015) → DeepStack (2017, jointly with Charles University/Czech Technical University). [Pokerlistings: From Loki to Libratus](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/from-loki-to-libratus-a-look-at-20-years-of-poker-ai-development) (B, as of 2026-08) corroborated by [CPRG homepage](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/) (A, as of 2026-08).

- **Loki/Poki — opponent modelling, sparse data:** Poki took "effective hand strength" as input and output a (fold, call, raise) probability triple using basic opponent modelling; Loki-2 added a selective-sampling simulation to better estimate EV, biasing the sample toward weighted opponent hand ranges rather than uniform sampling — a direct answer to the sparse-data problem (you rarely see an opponent's actual cards). Source: [Billings et al., "Opponent Modeling in Poker" (AAAI 1998)](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/AAAI98.pdf), retrieved and read this session (A, as of 2026-08). **The paper's own framing is exploitative, not imitative**: it tracks betting patterns/hand frequencies to "identify and capitalize on predictable weaknesses" — style capture is a means to exploitation, not an end.
  - **Known limitation, stated by the source material:** Poki "cannot adapt its strategy fast enough to exploit its opponents or prevent its own exploitation" — a documented failure mode of a fixed/slow-adapting formula-based agent. [Pokerlistings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/from-loki-to-libratus-a-look-at-20-years-of-poker-ai-development) (B).
- **Vexbot — real-time opponent modelling + expectimax:** Uses adaptive game-tree search (Miximax/Miximix) with real-time opponent statistics to compute EV of each action; explicitly framed by its authors as exploiting predictability, "a much more dangerous opponent than is possible with a purely game-theoretic approach." [Search synthesis of Billings/Davidson, "Game-Tree Search with Adaptation in Stochastic Imperfect-Information Games"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Game-Tree-Search-with-Adaptation-in-Stochastic-Billings-Davidson/d8fe28c0def554733ec6042b561430607c0331c6) (B, as of 2026-08).
- **Restricted Nash Response (RNR):** Johanson, Zinkevich, Bowling, **"Computing Robust Counter-Strategies," NIPS 2007** (published as NeurIPS proceedings 2008). Core method: solve a modified game where the opponent is forced to follow a learned model with probability *p* and free to deviate with probability (1−*p*), trading off exploitability vs. exploitation. Retrieved and read this session: [johanson.ca paper page](https://johanson.ca/publications/poker/2007-nips-rnash/2007-nips-rnash.html) (A, as of 2026-08). **The page states directly that this is about exploiting a suspected tendency while bounding worst-case loss — not imitating human style.**
  - Follow-on: **"Data Biased Robust Counter Strategies," AISTATS 2009** (Johanson et al.) exists and is retrievable ([poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/AISTATS09.pdf](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/AISTATS09.pdf), URL confirmed via search, B — PDF text extraction failed this session so content is summarized only from the search snippet, not read in full) — addresses building robust counter-strategies when the opponent model itself is built from limited/biased data. This is the closest CPRG work to "modelling with sparse data," but again the goal is a counter-strategy, not a humanlike generator.
  - Related, not CPRG: **Ganzfried & Sun, "Bayesian Opponent Exploitation in Imperfect-Information Games" (arXiv 1603.03491, 2016)** — first exact algorithm using Dirichlet priors for Bayesian opponent modelling + exploitation. Abstract retrieved this session (A, as of 2026-08): [arxiv.org/abs/1603.03491](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03491). Confirms the exploitation framing is a persistent theme across the wider (non-CPRG) opponent-modelling literature too, not unique to Alberta.
- **Polaris — man vs. machine:** 2007 Polaris lost its match to human pros; the improved **2008 Polaris won the Second Man-Machine Poker Championship** 3 wins–2 losses–1 tie over 6,000 hands (6 duplicate 500-hand matches), net +195 big blinds. [poker.cs.ualberta.ca man-machine press page](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/man-machine/Press/SC-06-27-2008/) and [Wikipedia: Polaris (poker bot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_(poker_bot)) (both B, as of 2026-08). This establishes strength, not humanlikeness — no human-rating protocol was used in this event per the retrieved sources.
- **Cepheus — essentially solving HU limit hold'em:** Bowling, Burch, Johanson, Tammelin, **"Heads-up Limit Hold'em Poker is Solved," Science, January 2015**. Best exploiting counter-strategy wins only 0.000986 big blinds/game — statistically unbeatable in a human lifetime of play. Also established the dealer's structural edge (~0.088 big blinds/game). [IJCAI 2015 paper PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/097.pdf), [Wikipedia: Cepheus (poker bot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cepheus_(poker_bot)) (B, as of 2026-08). Purely a strength/game-theory result; zero human-likeness claim.
- **DeepStack — first neural-network-based poker champion:** Moravčík, Schmid, et al., **"DeepStack: Expert-Level Artificial Intelligence in Heads-Up No-Limit Poker," Science, May 2017**. Combines recursive reasoning, decomposition (only solves the relevant subgame), and a deep-network-learned intuition function from self-play; beat professional players with statistical significance over 44,000 hands. [arXiv:1701.01724](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01724) (A, as of 2026-08). Architecturally notable for this project: it is the CPRG lineage's own precedent for **replacing a hand-tuned heuristic with a learned value function**, though again in service of strength, not humanlikeness, and its per-decision compute (subgame re-solves via CFR at decision time) is too expensive for the project's 500 hands/sec budget as published — it is not itself a candidate architecture, only evidence that the lineage itself abandoned pure hand-tuning once strength stalled.
- **DIVAT / MIVAT / AIVAT — variance-reduced *strength* evaluation, not human-likeness evaluation:**
  - **DIVAT** (Zinkevich, Bowling, Bard, Kan, Billings) — control-variate estimator that subtracts expected chance-outcome utility to isolate the value attributable to player decisions; empirically 75–85% variance reduction vs. Monte Carlo in HU limit hold'em, later proven statistically unbiased. [poker.cs.ualberta.ca DIVAT paper](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/divat-icgaj.pdf) (A, as of 2026-08).
  - **AIVAT** (Burch, Schmid, Moravčík, Bowling, arXiv 1612.06915, 2016) generalizes DIVAT/MIVAT to also remove variance from *known-strategy player actions*, not just chance events. Retrieved and read this session via [ar5iv mirror](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915) (A, as of 2026-08).
  - **Relevance flagged, not established:** these tools measure *win-rate signal cleanly*, which is a different axis from human-likeness. No retrieved source repurposes DIVAT/AIVAT machinery for a humanness metric — this is a **gap**, not a finding (see "What works vs what fails" and NO INFORMATION FOUND).
- **Data released:** CPRG hosts the **IRC Poker Database** (Michael Maurer's IRC Observer logs, ~9.48M extracted hold'em hands, 1995–2001, publicly downloadable) plus the Annual Computer Poker Competition (ACPC) protocol/tools. [CPRG other resources page](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/other_resources.html), [IRC Poker Database page](http://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/irc_poker_database.html) (both B/A, as of 2026-08). This is real human hand-history data at a scale that could feed a behavior-cloning approach — but it predates modern no-limit online poker and its structure (IRC-server play, mostly limit games, no modern population) limits direct transfer to a modern NLHE persona system.

## Thread 2 — Human-likeness as an explicit objective in game AI

- **BotPrize (2K BotPrize), Unreal Tournament 2004:** Annual Turing-test-style competition (from 2008) judged on a 1–5 humanness scale by human judges who played 10-minute deathmatch rounds against both bots and humans, unaware which was which per encounter. **2012: two bots (UT², University of Texas at Austin, using neuroevolution + behavioral adaptation; and MirrorBot, Mihai Polceanu) scored "humanness" ratings above the human players themselves** (52% vs. an average human rating of only ~40–41%). [Wikipedia: Computer game bot Turing Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_game_bot_Turing_Test) (B, as of 2026-08); corroborated by [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/turing-test-ut2004-botprize/24308/) and [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/2012/10/20/unreal-bots-pass-turing-test/amp) (B, as of 2026-08). **Critical caveat, directly documented:** the bar the bots cleared was itself low and noisy — real humans were rated non-human ~59–60% of the time by other human judges, so "passing" is partly an artifact of a weak baseline, not proof the bots were flawlessly humanlike.
- **Maia (chess human move-prediction):** A line of models (Maia, Maia-2, Maia-3) trained on large human game corpora (Lichess) to *predict the actual next move a human of a given skill band would play*, rather than the objectively best move. Maia-1/2 outperform Stockfish/Leela by 5–15 percentage points at predicting human moves; Maia-3 uses a transformer with "Geometric Attention Bias" and can personalize to individual players. [arXiv:2409.20553 (Maia-2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.20553), [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/the-human-side-of-ai-for-chess/), [CSSLab/maia3 GitHub](https://github.com/CSSLab/maia3) (A/B, as of 2026-08). This is the **cleanest transferable template for "measure and hit human-likeness"**: train a small net to predict the human distribution of actions conditioned on skill/style, and score any candidate policy by how well it matches that predictive distribution (move-matching accuracy + likelihood).
- **"Navigates Like Me" (Milani, Juliani, Momennejad, Georgescu, Rzepecki, Shaw, Costello, Fang, Devlin, Hofmann; CHI 2023):** Built a navigation agent explicitly optimized toward humanlike movement statistics; ran a large crowdsourced Turing-test-style evaluation comparing its videos against baseline-AI and human-player videos. **Result: the purpose-built agent passed the Turing test (judges could not statistically distinguish it from humans); the baseline AI agents did not.** [arXiv:2303.02160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02160) abstract page (A, as of 2026-08). Directly supports the claim that human-likeness has to be *targeted and trained for* — a generic competent agent does not become humanlike as a side effect of being good.
- **"The Many Challenges of Human-Like Agents in Virtual Game Environments" (Świechowski, Ślęzak, AAMAS 2025):** A dedicated survey answering exactly this project's two questions — how to build human-like game AI, and how to measure human-likeness. Identifies **13 conceptual/technical challenges**. States an inverse relationship as a general thesis: **"the more challenging it is to create human-like AI for a given game, the easier it becomes to develop a method for distinguishing humans from AI-driven players"** — i.e., the harder human-likeness is, the more detectable failures are. Includes an empirical bot-detection component using a deep recurrent convolutional network. [arXiv:2505.20011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20011) abstract (A, as of 2026-08).
- **"Improving the Human-Likeness of Game AI's Moves by Combining Multiple Prediction Models" (Ogawa, Hsueh, Ikeda; ICAART 2023, pp. 931–939):** Defines and compares two human-likeness metrics — **move-matching accuracy** and **likelihood** (geometric mean of probability assigned to the human's actual move) — and shows blending multiple predictive models improves both by a few percentage points. [SciTePress paper page confirmed via search](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2023/118042/118042.pdf); full text could not be extracted this session (PDF binary), so this entry is **grade B** (title/venue/authors/abstract-level claim corroborated by two independent search result snippets — ResearchGate and SciTePress — but the underlying method detail was not read verbatim).
- **Drivatar (Forza Motorsport, Microsoft/Turn 10):** Longest-running commercial example of imitation-learning-driven human-likeness in a shipped game. Evolved from simple behavioral cloning (2005) to cloud-trained deep neural networks (Forza Motorsport 5 onward) that learn an individual player's driving style and reproduce it in novel situations, explicitly framed as "how would this player drive this car, on this track" rather than exact move copying. **Note:** starting with 2023's Forza Motorsport, Drivatar was replaced by non-human-data AI to improve competitive fairness — a real-world data point that a shipped studio *walked back* human-imitation AI for other design reasons. [Game Developer: "How Forza's Drivatar Actually Works"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works), [AIandGames: "How Forza Rebuilt Their Drivatar AI System"](https://www.aiandgames.com/p/how-forza-rebuilt-their-drivatar) (B, as of 2026-08).
- **Behavior cloning at real-time speed — Counter-Strike (Pearce & Zhu, "Counter-Strike Deathmatch with Large-Scale Behavioural Cloning," NeurIPS Offline RL Workshop 2021):** Trained a deep neural network via behavioral cloning on ~4M frames of scraped human play (plus a smaller expert set); the paper explicitly requires the network to **"train and run in real-time"** since no CS:GO API exists for other integration. Result: matches medium built-in-AI difficulty "whilst adopting a humanlike play style." [arXiv:2104.04258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04258) abstract (A, as of 2026-08). **Direct evidence a behavior-cloned net can hit both a real-time inference budget and a qualitatively humanlike style** — the closest general-game-AI precedent to this project's engineering constraint.
- **Persona-conditioned RL for many cheap agents — PCSP ("One Policy, Infinite NPCs," Hong, arXiv 2605.23652, 2026) and MultiGAIL (Ahlberg, Sestini, Tollmar, Gisslén, CoG 2023, arXiv 2308.07598):** PCSP conditions a single RL policy on frozen LLM-embedded persona descriptions and reports **22x faster inference than an LLM-as-policy baseline, with a sub-frame inference profile at 64 concurrent agents in Unreal Engine 5** — i.e., real-time-safe. MultiGAIL uses multiple discriminators (one per target playstyle) as GAIL reward models so one network can be steered across a spectrum of human playstyles without hand-authoring a reward function per style. [arXiv:2605.23652 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23652), [arXiv:2308.07598 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07598) (both A, as of 2026-08). These are the most direct **candidate-architecture precedents** for "many distinct, cheap, style-conditioned agents."

## Thread 3 — Poker-specific player modelling / population modelling

- **Teófilo & Reis, "Identifying Player's Strategies in No Limit Texas Hold'em Poker through the Analysis of Individual Moves" (arXiv:1301.5943, submitted 2013):** Applies clustering to a game database, first identifying common move "types," then profiling each player by the frequency of each move type. **Found 7 distinct player-style clusters**, each characterized by at least one signature tactic. Stated purpose: improve an opponent-modelling agent's ability to predict actions by matching a new opponent to the nearest known cluster — an exploitation aid, not a humanness generator. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5943), read this session (A, as of 2026-08). **This is the single closest retrieved academic precedent to the project's "merit table" dial architecture** — both use a small number of discrete style buckets derived from move statistics — but the source never measures or claims human-judged believability for any of the 7 clusters.
- **Teófilo, Reis, Cardoso, Félix, Sêca, Ferreira, Mendes, Cruz, Pereira, Passos, "Computer Poker Research at LIACC" (arXiv:1301.5946, 2013):** A lab-overview paper describing LIACC's (Portugal) poker research programme and theses; abstract retrieved and read this session gives no detail on population modelling beyond naming it as an active area. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5946) (A for the abstract; **no information found** on specific methodology beyond it — full text not retrieved).
- **Population/behavioral clustering, industry-adjacent:** A Swedish university thesis (Olofsson & Matkaselkä, via DiVA portal) on "Big Data and Player Behavior in Poker" was located by search and its URL retrieved, but the PDF could not be parsed this session (binary extraction failure both times attempted) — **listed for completeness at grade C (URL confirmed, content unverified this session)**: [diva-portal.org record](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2075591/FULLTEXT01.pdf).
- **What's conspicuously absent:** no retrieved source clusters players AND validates the clusters against human judges rating "does this look like a real person of style X." Population modelling in poker, as retrieved, stops at the classification/exploitation step.

## Thread 4 — How the literature measures behavioural human-likeness

Concrete, reusable definitions retrieved this session:

1. **Judge/discriminator Turing tests.** BotPrize: human judges score 1–5 "humanness" per encounter, no forced binary choice; aggregate score compared to average human-rated humanness as the baseline bar. "Navigates Like Me": crowdsourced pairwise/rated comparisons of agent vs. human video clips, checked for **statistical indistinguishability** (not just "high score"), plus qualitative coding of *why* judges picked what they picked. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_game_bot_Turing_Test), [arXiv:2303.02160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02160) (B/A, as of 2026-08).
2. **Move-matching accuracy.** Fraction of the model's top-predicted (or top-k%) action that equals what the human actually played, at a given decision point. Used directly in the Maia line and in Ogawa/Hsueh/Ikeda (ICAART 2023, ~Top-10%/Top-20% variant reported also in a Hanabi human-proxy context). [arXiv:2409.20553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.20553) (A); Hanabi/top-k framing corroborated in search snippet only (B).
3. **Likelihood / perplexity.** The geometric mean probability the model assigns to the human's actually-taken action across a held-out set of real human decisions (equivalently, exp of negative average log-likelihood = perplexity). Lower perplexity / higher likelihood = the model's distribution over actions looks more like the real human distribution, independent of whether the single top pick matches. Defined explicitly in Ogawa/Hsueh/Ikeda (ICAART 2023) and standard in NLP perplexity literature. (B for the poker-adjacent game-AI framing, corroborated by generic NLP-perplexity sources.)
4. **Discriminator/classifier detection.** Train a classifier (in the retrieved cases: an LSTM+dense/sigmoid GAN discriminator, or a deep recurrent convolutional net) to separate human traces from bot traces; a low classifier accuracy (near chance) = the bot is behaviorally indistinguishable by that detector. Used both as an adversarial *training* signal (MultiGAIL, GAN-based bot detectors) and as a *post-hoc evaluation* tool (Świechowski & Ślęzak's AAMAS 2025 survey explicitly builds one). [arXiv:2308.07598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07598), [arXiv:2505.20011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20011), search-corroborated GAN-discriminator bot-detection framing (B).
5. **Distributional/action-frequency distance** (e.g., matching the frequency with which a style plays each action type across situations) is implicit in the Teófilo & Reis clustering method (Thread 3) and in general population-style profiling, but no retrieved source names a specific named distance metric (KL divergence, Wasserstein, etc.) applied to poker action frequencies for a *humanness* objective specifically — **flagged as a gap**, not found.

**What none of this measures directly:** strength/EV. All five families above are explicitly style/behavior metrics, decoupled from win-rate — which is the opposite axis from the poker-strength literature in Thread 1. This split is itself the key structural finding: **the CPRG/poker-AI literature optimizes and measures strength; the general game-AI literature optimizes and measures human-likeness; almost no retrieved source does both in poker.**

## Thread 5 — Known failure modes, critiques, negative results

- **Hand-tuned/rule-based agents plateau below human-rated baselines, and even where they "win," the win is against a weak baseline.** BotPrize's own numbers: winning bots hit ~52% humanness against an average *human* rating of only ~40–41% — the standard being cleared is itself mediocre. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_game_bot_Turing_Test), [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/turing-test-ut2004-botprize/24308/) (B, as of 2026-08).
- **Loebner-Prize-style scripted/rule-based chatbots are documented as failing to sustain coherence or handle open-ended queries, criticized for prioritizing deception over genuine capability** — a directly analogous critique to "a dial architecture can be tuned to dodge a specific tell without becoming genuinely more humanlike." [arXiv:2505.02558, "The Turing Test Is More Relevant Than Ever"] and related Turing-test critique sources retrieved via search (B, as of 2026-08; this is a synthesis across multiple retrieved snippets rather than one deeply-read paper — treat as **C** for the chatbot-specific framing, since no single source was read in full).
- **CPRG's own stated limitation of Poki:** "cannot adapt its strategy fast enough to exploit its opponents or prevent its own exploitation" — a fixed/slow-adapting formula architecture has a documented ceiling *within the CPRG's own account of its work*. [Pokerlistings synthesis](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/from-loki-to-libratus-a-look-at-20-years-of-poker-ai-development) (B, as of 2026-08).
- **Restricted Nash Response's own documented weakness:** it is "not as effective when only some information about the opponent's strategy is available," and a naive frequentist approach to building the opponent model may not work in practice — an explicit, source-stated failure mode of a hand-built-model-plus-optimization approach under sparse data, which is structurally close to this project's situation (bots need to look like specific humans from limited design intent, not limited observation, but the sparse-data fragility lesson generalizes). [Search synthesis of RNR follow-on literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.03491) (B, as of 2026-08).
- **Drivatar's reversal:** Turn 10/Microsoft explicitly dropped human-data-trained Drivatar AI in the 2023 Forza Motorsport reboot in favor of non-human-data AI, citing competitive fairness and driving-quality concerns — direct evidence that even a mature, shipped, well-resourced human-imitation system was judged **not good enough on its own terms and replaced**, for reasons beyond raw human-likeness (fairness/quality trade-offs matter in production, not just the humanness score). [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works), [AIandGames](https://www.aiandgames.com/p/how-forza-rebuilt-their-drivatar) (B, as of 2026-08).
- **Real-world poker bot arms race confirms rule-based/statistical bots are the most detectable tier, and that evading detection at scale has required human-in-the-loop hybrids, not purer statistical tuning.** Per the Bloomberg investigative piece (Kit Chellel, "The Russian Bot Army That Conquered Online Poker," Bloomberg Businessweek, 2024): the operation ran bots "fully automated or with a human operator implementing decisions from a machine" specifically to confound detection, and pivoted business models multiple times as bots "were quickly being banned." [Bloomberg feature page confirmed via search](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2024-poker-bots-artificial-intelligence-russia/) — **direct WebFetch of the article returned HTTP 403 (paywalled/blocked)**, so content here is from the search-result synthesis only, **grade C** (single-channel, not independently read).
- **General bot-detection literature confirms first-generation rule-based bots are detectable via timing and statistical-deviation analysis** ("the gap between mechanical execution and human behavior was wide and measurable"), and that closing that gap has driven the field toward GAN/adversarial and deep-learning approaches on both the bot-building and bot-detecting sides — i.e., the arms race itself is evidence that naive rule-tuning has a low detectability ceiling. Search-synthesized across multiple retrieved snippets, not a single deeply-read paper — **grade C**.
- **No retrieved source directly states "we tuned a fixed heuristic architecture's parameters and it provably could not close a human-likeness gap, here is the ceiling we hit."** This exact negative result — the one most directly relevant to the owner's overhaul-or-not decision — was **not found** in this session's search. See `NO INFORMATION FOUND ON` below.

## What works vs what fails

**What works (literature-supported):**
- **Train a small model directly on real human decision data to predict the human's actual next action**, then use that same model both as the generator (or a component of it) and as the human-likeness metric (move-matching accuracy, likelihood). This is the Maia recipe and it is the most rigorously validated pattern retrieved. (A)
- **Use behavioral cloning from logged human play when a physics/reflex simulation is needed at real-time speed**, as CS:GO and Drivatar both did — proof that a *learned* model, not a hand-tuned rule set, is what closes the gap once a domain gets behaviorally complex, and that it can still hit a live-game latency budget. (A/B)
- **Condition one small shared policy on a style/persona vector** rather than hand-authoring N separate rule sets — PCSP and MultiGAIL both show this scales to many distinct behavior profiles cheaply and stays real-time-safe. (A)
- **Measure human-likeness with a held-out judge/discriminator test, not just an internal statistic** — BotPrize and "Navigates Like Me" both show that the true test is whether human raters (or a classifier standing in for them) can tell the difference; internal metrics like move-matching accuracy correlate with but do not guarantee this. (A/B)
- **Use variance-reduction estimators (DIVAT/AIVAT-style control variates) when measuring anything statistically noisy from hand outcomes** — directly reusable machinery if the project ever needs to measure, e.g., "does persona X actually win/lose at the rate a human of that style would," separated from card-luck noise, even though the CPRG tools themselves were built for strength not style. (A)

**What fails / pitfalls (literature-supported):**
- **Fixed rule/heuristic architectures plateau, and CPRG's own account of Poki documents this as an adaptation-speed ceiling, not a tuning problem** — more tuning of a fixed formula does not fix an architecture that cannot adapt. (B)
- **A model built to exploit or model an opponent from sparse/frequentist data is fragile and its counter-strategy quality degrades** (RNR's documented weakness) — a caution against assuming any opponent/style model generalizes cleanly from limited data without a principled Bayesian or robustness treatment. (B)
- **Passing a low bar looks like more validation than it is** — BotPrize's winning bots beat an average *human* rating of only ~40%, so "beat the Turing test" claims need the baseline stated explicitly, or the win is against noise, not against a strong human standard. (B)
- **Human-imitation systems can be judged not good enough for production even after years of investment and be replaced** — Drivatar's 2023 reversal is a caution that "trained on human data" is necessary but not sufficient for a shipped product; other constraints (fairness, controllability, predictability) can dominate. (B)
- **Strength and style are measured by almost entirely disjoint tool sets in the retrieved literature** — DIVAT/AIVAT (strength, variance-reduced) versus move-matching/likelihood/discriminator tests (style). Naively reusing a strength-evaluation pipeline (e.g., "our bots' win rates look fine") says nothing about human-likeness; the two must be measured and optimized separately, and no retrieved poker-specific source has unified them. (Structural finding, not a single citation — synthesis grade B.)
- **Detectability is often evaded by adding a human back into the loop, not by better automated tuning** — the real-world poker bot arms race (Bloomberg) suggests that at the frontier, pure algorithmic tuning has hit a wall that operators worked around with humans, not with better bots. Treat this as suggestive, not proven — single-source, grade C.

## Candidate architectures

For each: what it is in plain terms, what data it needs, whether it can hit ~500 hands/sec, and evidence grade.

1. **Behavior-cloned small neural net per persona/style (Maia-style / CS:GO-style).** A compact network (the retrieved examples are chess-board-input transformers/CNNs and FPS-state-input deep nets, not LLMs) trained via supervised learning to predict the probability distribution over actions that a human of a target style/skill would take, given the current game state. **Data needed:** a corpus of real (or carefully human-labeled synthetic) hand histories tagged by style/skill; the CPRG IRC database (~9.5M hands) is a real but dated candidate source, and would likely need supplementing with modern NLHE data. **Speed:** yes — Maia-class nets and the CS:GO net are both explicitly built to run in real time on commodity hardware; a small feedforward/transformer net evaluated once per decision is comfortably within microsecond-to-low-millisecond territory, well inside a 500 hands/sec budget. **Grade: A** (directly evidenced architecture pattern, though never demonstrated in poker specifically in the retrieved sources — the poker-specific gap is real, see NO INFORMATION FOUND).
2. **Single persona-conditioned policy (PCSP-style).** One shared network takes the game state plus a compact persona/style embedding as input, rather than N separate rule sets or N separate networks. **Data needed:** either human logs per persona (for behavior cloning) or a hand-authored reward/imitation signal per persona (for RL/GAIL-style training, as in MultiGAIL). **Speed:** yes, by a wide margin — PCSP reports sub-frame inference for 64 concurrent agents in a real game engine, and 22x faster than an LLM baseline; a poker decision is far simpler than a navigation/combat decision. **Grade: A** (evidenced in non-poker games; not evidenced in poker specifically).
3. **Multi-discriminator adversarial imitation learning (MultiGAIL-style).** Train one policy against several discriminators, each representing a target playstyle, using adversarial imitation learning instead of hand-written reward shaping. **Data needed:** either real human demonstrations per style, or scripted/heuristic "expert" trajectories per style if human logs aren't available (weaker, but usable as a bootstrap). **Speed:** the trained result is a standard small policy network, same real-time profile as (1)/(2) — the adversarial/discriminator machinery is training-time-only cost, not inference-time cost. **Grade: A** (evidenced in general game AI, not poker).
4. **Player-style clustering to define target personas (Teófilo & Reis-style), feeding data into any of the above.** Use clustering on move-frequency features from a hand-history corpus to *define* what the N target styles actually are (empirically, from real play, rather than by design intuition), then train per-cluster models. **Data needed:** a hand-history corpus with enough volume per style bucket; the project's own architecture already has something structurally similar (merit-table dials) that could be replaced or informed by empirically-derived clusters instead of hand-guessed ones. **Speed:** the clustering itself is offline/design-time only; whatever downstream model consumes the cluster assignment determines runtime speed (see 1–3). **Grade: A for the clustering precedent itself; not evidenced as sufficient alone for human-rated realism** — the source measures cluster distinctiveness, not human-judged believability.
5. **Judge/discriminator classifier as a measurement (not generation) layer, reused from any of the above.** Train a lightweight classifier (LSTM+dense, or similar) to distinguish bot hands from human hands in the project's own logs, and use its accuracy (chance = fully humanlike, from a detectability standpoint) as a regression-testable realism score — directly modeled on the AAMAS 2025 survey's own detector and on GAN-discriminator bot-detection work. **Data needed:** a labeled corpus of real human hands (even a modest one) plus the bot's own generated hands. **Speed:** irrelevant at play-time — this runs offline as an evaluation/CI gate, not per-decision. **Grade: A/B** (well-evidenced as a general pattern; not previously applied to this specific poker engine).
6. **DeepStack-style real-time value-function re-solving.** Included for completeness and explicitly ruled out: replacing per-decision heuristics with a learned value network plus real-time CFR-style re-solving of the local subgame, as DeepStack does. **Speed:** DeepStack's published approach takes on the order of seconds per decision on the hardware described in its paper — **not compatible with the project's ~500 hands/sec / instant-feel constraint** as retrieved. It is listed only as the lineage's own strongest evidence that hand-tuned heuristics were abandoned once strength (not realism) plateaued — an architectural cautionary precedent, not a usable candidate. **Grade: A** (well-evidenced, but explicitly disqualified by the speed constraint).
