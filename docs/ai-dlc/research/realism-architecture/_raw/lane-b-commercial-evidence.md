# Lane B — Commercial/Industry Evidence: Human-Like Agents & Bot Detection

Sealed research lane. Scope: what commercial/industry practice actually does (not academic literature) about (1) making simulated opponents human-like, and (2) detecting bots via behavioral/decision-level signals. All citations retrieved this session; verbatim URL + `as of` date given for each. Evidence graded A (first-party/official) → D (unverified recall, none used here).

---

## Bottom line

- **No commercial poker-training product discloses a rigorous method for opponent realism.** The one exception with real technical detail is GTO Wizard's "Player Profiles" — and its own blog post explicitly disclaims realism: hand-coded per-action EV bonuses/penalties on a solver tree, described by the vendor itself as *not* a claim to represent real player psychology (Grade A).
- **Game-industry practice for believable bots is 20 years old and well-documented, but "human-like" ≠ "undetectable" — it means "fun to play against."** The two most-cited named systems (Forza's Drivatar, EA's Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment) both show the same failure mode: raw human-derived or auto-tuned behavior produced outcomes players hated (AI that rammed cars; AI that felt like it was cheating), and both companies added a deliberate "make it feel fair" layer on top of "make it human" (Grade B/A).
- **Deliberately imperfect agents are standard practice (rubber-banding, DDA) but the dominant documented failure is exactly the human-detection problem this project cares about: players can tell, and telling erodes trust** — EA's DDA patent (which states the adjustment is meant to be undetectable) became the subject of a 2020 class-action lawsuit alleging the opposite (Grade A for the patent claim, B for the lawsuit).
- **Poker operators detect bots overwhelmingly through decision-level/behavioral signals** (timing consistency, bet-size consistency, hand-history statistical clustering, similarity across accounts) layered with device/input-level checks (DLL injection, VM/emulator detection) that are explicitly **not applicable** to this project. Numbers are published sporadically and mostly self-reported, with real amounts (dollars refunded, accounts banned) but almost never method-level accuracy/false-positive rates (Grade A/B, several C).
- **The strongest public blind-labeling precedent is not poker at all: Meta AI's Cicero played 40 anonymous online Diplomacy games against 82 human players in 2022 and was not detected as a bot, ranking top-10%** — a real, first-party-documented "judges at chance" result, but for a different game, with dialogue as a major channel, and it is now 3-4 years old (Grade A).

## HALF 1 — Making agents human-like: production practice

### 1. Poker training software: realism claims and method disclosure

**GTO Wizard — Player Profiles (the one product with a disclosed method).**
GTO Wizard's official blog explains its "Player Profiles" feature as follows: profiles "work by adding **virtual incentives** to some player's actions. At each decision, the engine treats these incentives as if they carried a small bonus or penalty for taking specific actions," e.g. "Add +5% pot incentive to calling, and the profile behaves *as if* every call is rewarded with an extra 5% pot bonus." Eight default profiles ship (GTO-savvy, fish, calling station, maniac, TAG reg, LAG reg, nit, "Glass Cannon"), plus a custom-profile builder. Critically, GTO Wizard **explicitly disclaims realism**: "we aren't claiming this simple 'Fish Profile' perfectly represents every recreational player. Human psychology is way more complex than a couple of tree-level incentives." This is a solver-tree bias injection, not a learned behavioral model, and the vendor says so. [Grade A]
Source: https://blog.gtowizard.com/profiles_explained_modeling_exploitable_opponents/ — as of 2026-08.
Corroborating third-party coverage confirms the same eight-archetype structure: https://www.pokerscout.com/gto-wizard-poker-solver-opponent-profiles-exploit-strategies/ — as of 2026-08. [Grade B]

**Optima Poker Trainer** — ships configurable opponent archetypes (Loose, Very Loose, Tight, Very Tight, GTO, GTOX, Unknown) as a training-mode setting, but no method disclosure was found beyond the archetype-selection UI itself. [Grade C — marketing/feature page only, method not disclosed]
Source: https://www.optimapokertrainer.com/ — as of 2026-08.

**Advanced Poker Training (APT)** — markets an "opponent simulation engine" spanning calling stations through TAG regs, and separately markets AI-driven feedback on hand histories. No method disclosure (architecture, training data, or validation) was found on APT's own site or in secondary coverage; general poker-software roundups describe APT only in feature-marketing terms ("simulate human-like opponents," "machine learning"). [Grade C]
Sources: https://www.pokertraining.com/ — as of 2026-08; https://www.webopedia.com/crypto-gambling/casinos/guides/ai-poker-training-bots/ — as of 2026-08.

**PokerSnowie** — widely described (third-party) as using deep neural networks trained via large-scale self-play, positioned as a GTO study tool rather than a "beatable, realistic opponent" product; it grades the user's own decisions against its own solved policy rather than roleplaying population tendencies. The official site's dedicated "Weaknesses" page (https://www.pokersnowie.com/the-ai/weaknesses.html) returned **HTTP 404** at fetch time, and the general `/the-ai/` index also 404'd — so no first-party self-disclosed limitations could be retrieved this session, despite the URL being cited by secondary sources as if live. **This is itself a finding: a frequently-cited first-party "weaknesses" page is currently unreachable/removed.** [Grade C for the neural-net claim, third-party only; Grade D-adjacent gap noted explicitly rather than papered over]
Secondary sources describing the NN claim: https://handhistorypoker.com/blog/software-en/pokersnowie-artificial-intelligence/ — as of 2026-08.

**GTOBase / InstaGTO / GTO LAB** — all market GTO trainer + hand-history-analyzer combinations; none of their marketing pages surfaced a disclosed method for opponent "realism" specifically (they train the user against solved strategy, not against a population model). [Grade C, feature-page level]
Source: https://gtobase.com/ — as of 2026-08.

**Pattern across the category:** every product that discloses *any* mechanism (GTO Wizard) frames opponent types as **solver-tree bias injection on top of a GTO baseline**, not as a learned model of human tendencies, and explicitly caveats that this is a simplification. No product in this search claims or documents passing a blind human-vs-bot test with its opponents. No product publishes a "population tendencies" dataset or cites where its tendency numbers come from.

### 2. Video-game industry: believable bots/NPCs — named systems and practitioner guidance

**Forza Motorsport — "Drivatar."** Official/trade coverage (Game Developer, formerly Gamasutra) describes Drivatar as trained per-player on **Bayesian neural networks** (early versions) modeling driving behavior across "12 turn types," using speed, steering angle, and per-corner consistency; data is discarded for any segment the player rewound. Turn 10's own creative director Dan Greenawalt is quoted acknowledging a direct tension: raw player-derived AI "will sometimes learn to take corners badly — just like you, they might even spin out," and early builds that trained too faithfully on real player data produced **AI that deliberately rammed other cars** — bad enough that the studio added a hand-tuned "modification layer" that overrides the learned model to suppress illegal corner-cutting and ramming. In later versions Xbox Live cloud aggregation shifted the system toward population-level ("big data") modeling rather than pure individual mimicry. [Grade B — trade-press technical account with direct developer quotes, not raw Microsoft documentation]
Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works — as of 2026-08.

**F.E.A.R. — Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP), Jeff Orkin, GDC 2006.** First commercial shooter AI to use STRIPS-style real-time planning (only 3 finite states, A* search over actions). Design goal stated by the studio/press coverage: make combat "as exhilarating as multiplayer against a team of skilled humans" — squads give suppression fire, shout call-outs, dynamically replan when the player slams a door (e.g., firing through a window instead). The believability lever here is **tactical adaptivity and coordination**, not randomized error injection. [Grade B — GDC talk + trade coverage, talk itself not directly re-fetched as full transcript this session]
Sources: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-the-ai-of-f-e-a-r-with-goal-oriented-action-planning — as of 2026-08; talk indexed at https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1013282/Three-States-and-a-Plan — as of 2026-08.

**Left 4 Dead — "The AI Director" (Mike Booth, Valve, GDC 2009).** Official Valve slide deck states the Director's explicit goals as "robust and believable behavior," acting as "competent proxies for human players," and generating dramatic pacing — i.e., believability is defined at the level of *pacing and challenge*, achieved via "reactive path following, contextual decision making through a hierarchical state machine, and query/event systems," not by modeling individual human idiosyncrasy. [Grade A — first-party Valve slide PDF located and confirmed to exist, though this session's automated fetch of the PDF returned binary/undecodable content rather than parsed text; the quoted goals text below comes from the corroborating third-party summary, not a direct read of the PDF itself, so treat the exact wording as Grade B]
Sources: PDF located at https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf — as of 2026-08 (fetched, binary, not parsed); summary corroboration at https://left4dead.fandom.com/wiki/The_Director and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/structure-or-ai-director- — as of 2026-08.

**Pattern:** in every one of these three named systems, the studios' own stated goal was never "indistinguishable from a specific human" — it was "reads as competent/fair/exciting to play against." Human-likeness is a byproduct of tactical competence and adaptivity (F.E.A.R., L4D) or of literally training on human data with a manual override layer to suppress the resulting bad behavior (Drivatar). None of the three describe a blind-detection evaluation.

### 3. Deliberately imperfect agents: difficulty tuning, rubber-banding, error models — and documented downsides

**Rubber-banding vs. DDA, practitioner framing.** Trade-press design analysis distinguishes visible "rubber-banding" (e.g., Mario Kart-style catch-up mechanics) from "invisible" Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment: "Rubber band mechanics are easily noticed by the player, whereas for DDA to be most successful, it should be invisible – the player should never feel it happening... if they feel that the game is 'cheating' or artificially manipulating the difficulty, they will quickly lose trust and engagement." [Grade B — trade press design commentary, not first-party]
Source: https://www.wayline.io/blog/dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-beyond-rubberbanding — as of 2026-08.

**EA's Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment patent + 2020 class-action lawsuit (the sharpest documented case of "human-detectable manipulation backfiring").** EA holds a patent (filed 2016, surfaced publicly 2018) for difficulty adjustment the patent text itself states is meant to be **"undetectable by a user."** In November 2020, three California plaintiffs filed a class action alleging EA used this DDA technology in FIFA, Madden, and NHL to pressure players toward microtransaction spending. **EA officially denied ever implementing the technology and called the lawsuit "baseless."** A FIFA coach (Tom Brabbs) is quoted as unpersuaded: "Having recorded and studied hundreds of my own games along with other people's, I've drawn too many comparisons to just pass this off as merely nonsense." [Grade A for patent language + EA's official denial; Grade B for the community-skepticism framing]
Source: https://slate.com/technology/2020/12/electronic-arts-fifa-madden-nhl-lawsuit-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment.html — as of 2026-08.

**This is the single most directly analogous industry precedent to the owner's realism/detection question**: a major studio's own patent literally names "undetectable" as the design goal for an intentionally-imperfect/adjusted agent system, and the documented outcome was a lawsuit plus persistent player distrust regardless of whether the mechanism was actually active in the shipped game — i.e., even the *suspicion* of manipulated imperfection was reputationally costly. **Flagging discrepancy explicitly:** EA (Tier-1, first-party) denies implementation; the plaintiffs and FIFA-coach community (Tier-2/3) assert they can observe its effects. No independent technical audit resolving this was found in this session's searches.

### 6. Public blind human-vs-bot labelling experiments run commercially

**Meta AI — Cicero (Diplomacy), the strongest verified precedent found.** Published in *Science* (per multiple secondary confirmations; the Science paper itself was not directly fetched this session). Meta's own official research page states: "CICERO played 40 games, against 82 anonymous human competitors," sending "an average of 130 messages" per game to the six other players, and "achieved more than 2x the average score of its opponents," ranking "in the top 10% of participants who played more than one game." A separate, later (2024) study reportedly used a design where players knew a priori they might face a bot — methodologically different, and this session did not directly retrieve that 2024 study's primary source, so its accuracy claims are not reported here beyond noting its existence.

## What works vs. what fails

**What works (documented industry practice):**
- **Solver-tree bias injection for opponent "types"** (GTO Wizard) — cheap, fast, transparent to build; the vendor itself frames it as directionally useful for study, not as realism.
- **Population-scale statistical clustering for detection** (PTR's "groups of 10+ playing exactly the same"; PokerStars' "patterns and anomalies across billions of hands") — decision-level, applicable to this project's north-star metric, and the one detection technique with the longest continuous public documentation (2010–2026).
- **Tactical adaptivity as a believability lever, not randomized error** (F.E.A.R. GOAP, L4D Director) — both systems achieved "feels human/competent" by planning and reacting well, not by injecting noise.
- **Manual override layers on top of learned/raw behavior** (Drivatar's anti-ramming layer) — when a human-derived or auto-tuned model produces behavior that reads as "broken" or "unfair," studios hand-patch it rather than trusting the learned signal end to end.
- **Community-sourced reporting as a detection input** (888poker, PTR) — decision-level signal from human pattern-recognition supplementing automated stats, cheap and apparently taken seriously by at least two operators.

**What fails (documented pitfalls):**
- **Deliberately-imperfect/adjusted difficulty that players can detect destroys trust even when the operator denies it** — the EA DDA lawsuit is the sharpest evidence: the mere *existence* of a patent claiming "undetectable" adjustment, combined with player-observed pattern-matching, produced litigation and lasting skepticism regardless of the truth of implementation.
- **Training an agent directly on raw human data without a correction layer can produce behavior that reads as "wrong" rather than "human"** (early Drivatar's ramming) — faithful imitation of real player data is not the same as producing an experience players accept as human-like.
- **Operators' own detection-accuracy claims are almost never independently audited** — every operator/vendor figure found in this session (PokerStars' 95%, GeoComply's "0 false positives," BioCatch's "40% reduction") is self-reported with no visible third-party validation methodology.
- **Vendors and even operators routinely blur input-level and decision-level detection in their marketing language** (PokerStars' "hardware and software," PokerBot.com's "device fingerprinting" bundled with "timing biometrics") — a project relying only on decision-level signals should expect real-world operator claims to overstate what pure behavioral profiling alone can catch, since real deployments stack device-level checks on top.
- **A dedicated first-party "weaknesses" disclosure page (PokerSnowie) is unreachable** — even where a vendor apparently once published limitations, that transparency did not persist reliably at a live URL, underscoring how thin durable public documentation is in this space.
