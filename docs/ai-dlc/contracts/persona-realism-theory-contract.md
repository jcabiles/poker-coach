## 2. The softmax law (NON-NEGOTIABLE — the anti-cosmetic-change rule)

The engine clamps each candidate merit ≥0, **normalizes by the sum**, then draws via `rng.choices`. **A merit multiplier is therefore NOT the observed frequency change.** (audit §10.0 "How to read EVERY number"; RECONCILE cross-package note 1; roadmap "Softmax law").

Concrete re-derivation (RECONCILE RP1-S1 / audit §10.0): a 1.2×/0.75× IP/OOP merit on a TAG mid-pair (base P(bet) ≈ 0.507) yields an **observed** split of only **~1.27×**, NOT the "1.5–1.8×" a naive odds-space read expects. A ×0.50 overcard-damp merit on a TAG mid-pair yields **~34%** observed bet-rate, not 25%; on TOP_PAIR (base 74.6%) → **~59.5%**, barely −15pts (RECONCILE RP2-S2 / audit §10.1-P2c).

Two binding consequences:
1. **Every magnitude is a FIT SEED, tuned to a target measured stat — never a drop-in constant.** State the target as an observed closed-loop stat (a CBet split, an AF, a WTSD), then fit the multiplier to hit it. A quoted factor is a *starting shape/direction*, not the answer.
2. **Strong/saturated buckets barely move.** Normalization means a big multiplier on an already-dominant candidate (top-pair P(bet) ≈ 0.95) shifts the observed rate a few points at most. **The effect lives in the marginal / air / draw region.**

**Failure mode (a reviewer MUST reject this):** dropping `×0.75 / ×0.50` (P2) or a flat `×0.25` fold reduction (P6) into the code *as-is* and closing the slice ships a **cosmetic** change — the observed stat has not been fit. No slice closes on "the constant is in the code"; it closes on "the observed stat hit its target band" (roadmap D7 metric-DoD gate).

## 3. Semi-bluff EV identities (`[SOLVED]` — confirmed by independent re-derivation, RECONCILE RP3 / audit §10.1-P3a)

Jam `B` into pot `P`, where **P = pot BEFORE the jam** (see §7 denominator unification), equity-when-called `E`.

- **T1 — value-commit threshold** (zero fold-equity is OK; jam is committable):
  `E ≥ B / (P + 2B)`
- **T2 — required realized fold equity** (when below T1):
  `F* = (B − E·(P+2B)) / (P + B − E·(P+2B))`, collapsing to the classic `B/(P+B)` at `E=0`.
- **T3 — pure-call break-even:** `E ≥ B / (P+B)`. Note **T1 < T3 always** (a jam is committable at lower equity than a call).

**Stack-coverage caveat (CT-1, wave-6 lane C theory, 2026-08-02) — T1/T2 assume the villain covers `B`.**
When the villain's remaining stack cannot cover the jam (which is the case at **every re-raise-jam node** — the
re-raiser's `B` includes chips the original bettor no longer has behind), the exact break-even must be computed on
the **CAPPED final pot**: effective `B` = the amount the villain can actually call, not the hero's full jam.
Worked instance (T-M4, maniac 77-99 vs 4-bet): hero-behind 90.1 with villain holding only 76.24 gives a true T1
threshold of **0.447**, not the uncapped 0.419. Uncapped T1/T2 at such a node understates the threshold — cite the
capped form or state explicitly that the uncapped figure is a lower bound.

**Realization scope line (CT-2, wave-6 lane C theory, 2026-08-02) — the identities assume all-in equity
realization.** T1/T2/T3 price an ALL-IN: equity realizes fully because no further betting exists. At a node that
**leaves postflop play behind** (e.g. a call leaving SPR ≈ 1.5, OOP), T3 bounds a **RAW price only**, and any
slice citing it there must **state a realization discount** (equity × realization factor vs the price) rather than
present the raw T3 pass as sufficient. A gate may stay DIRECTIONAL if the discount is unquantified — but the
assumption must be stated, never silent. (T-M4's shipped behavior survives any plausible discount:
`.36 × .80 = .288 > .283`.)

**Corrected 3×-pot threshold (RECONCILE RP3 "T1 3×-POT EXAMPLE ARITHMETIC — WRONG" / audit §10.1-P3c):**
B = 3P ⇒ `3P/(P+6P) = 3/7 = ` **42.9%**, NOT 60%. The 60% figure is the α / fold-ceiling for a 1.5× bet, not T1 for a 3× jam. **RECONCILE corrected this — 42.9% is authoritative; never write 60% for the 3×-pot T1.** The F5/F6 illustration survives: a flush draw at E ≈ 0.35 < 0.429 fails T1 ⇒ keeps fold mass — but the number reads 42.9%.

**Bettor bluff-share formula (RECONCILE RP5-S5 — lead OVERRULED Sol / audit §10.1-P5c):** the bettor's optimal bluff share of a value bet is
`s / (1 + 2s)` (where `s` = bet-as-fraction-of-pot).
This gives ½=25%, ⅔=28.6%, pot=33%, 2×=40%. **RECONCILE explicitly overruled Sol's accusation that RP5 used the defender form `s/(1+s)`** (which would give 33/40/50/66.7%). RP5's theory is CORRECT — do NOT "fix" it to the defender form. The only wording correction: the engine's `_BUCKET_BLUFF_SHARE` is 4 coarse representatives (SMALL=.20, MED=.27, LARGE=.32, OVERBET=.375), "directionally consistent with `s/(1+2s)`, coarsened to 4 representatives" — NOT "exact match at every size." No engine table change.

### A8 — the limits of the bluff-share formula on the value side (S3-T3, 2026-08-22)

**Bottom line. The bluff-share formula above prices only ONE side of the betting range: the engine has levers that set how often a bot bluffs, and NONE that make a bot's value betting respond to bet size or stack depth. This amendment records that absence, records what was measured about it, and — most importantly — records what was NOT measured, because a lever built against a raw reading of this gap was withdrawn on review for pushing bot play in the wrong direction. It also files the open item the review turned up, which is the opposite mechanism to the one that was tried.**

S3-T3 is ticket 3 of improvement slice 3 (the calldown slice) of the bot-realism flywheel, `docs/ai-dlc/tickets/flywheel-slice3-calldown.md`. "Stack-to-pot ratio" is the acting seat's remaining stack divided by the pot. A "capped" decision is one where the seat cannot wager its own largest authored bet size, because its stack is smaller than that size.

**1. The value side has no lever, and no size or stack term at all.** `_AGG_BASE` (`backend/app/domain/personas_postflop.py`) is indexed by strength bucket alone. Measured on the merit vectors themselves, with zero sampling variance, on a K-9-3 rainbow flop at a pot of 10 big blinds: **every persona's probability of betting a TOP_PAIR or a MIDDLE_PAIR is identical to twelve decimal places at a stack-to-pot ratio of 10 and at 0.3** — top pair reads 0.7458 for the TAG, 0.4231 for the nit and the passive fish, 0.3793 for the calling station, at every ratio in between. The only stack response anywhere on the value side is the commit block: at or below the pack's `spr_commit`, a hand of overpair rank or better has its bet and raise weights multiplied by 3.0 and its fold weight zeroed. That is one step at one threshold, on the two strongest buckets, and it does not touch top pair or middle pair at all.

**2. What was measured about capped decisions, RAW and NORMALISED.** `backend/tools/capped_composition_probe.py` (shipped by S3-T3) measures the composition of the realised betting range at capped versus uncapped decisions, both raw and divided by the identity's own target evaluated at the pot-fraction each wager was ACTUALLY made at. The normalisation is not optional: **smaller wagers warrant a smaller bluff share, so a lower RAW bluff share at capped decisions is partly what `s/(1+2s)` asks for**, and the raw figure alone is not readable. The measured figures are in `docs/ai-dlc/research/slice3-calldown/t3-report.md` §3.

**3. What was NOT measured, stated because an earlier draft of this amendment overstated it.** The residual gap that survives normalisation is **NOT decomposed** into its possible causes. Three are live and this repository has separated none of them: the size the wager was made at (which normalisation handles), **arrival** — which hands the seat actually holds at a capped decision, the `π` in the identity's numerator and denominator, a property of how the hand got there rather than of any policy — and **policy**, the conditional probability of betting given the hand. Separating arrival from policy needs a `π`-by-node table that does not exist. **A gap reported on this instrument is a gap, not a defect**, and no slice may cite it as evidence that a policy is wrong until the decomposition is built.

**4. The identity is a property of a betting RANGE, not of a decision.** The realised bluff share at a decision is `Σ_bluff π(cell)·P(bet | cell) ÷ Σ_all π(cell)·P(bet | cell)`. A merit-layer lever moves only `P(bet | cell)`. Two consequences bind future work: an acceptance criterion of the form "this decision's composition equals `s/(1+2s)`" is unsatisfiable by construction and must be rejected at review; and a pooled criterion must be measured across seeds or paired, never on one seed, because the single-seed standard error on the capped-decision statistic is larger than the effect an admissible merit-layer lever produces.

**5. OPEN ITEM for the re-anchor slice — the missing mechanism is a commitment SLOPE on top pair and middle pair, and it points the OPPOSITE way to the lever that was tried.** Item 1's flatness is the finding: as the stack-to-pot ratio falls toward zero, a top pair or middle pair is progressively more committed and the poker says its betting frequency should RISE toward 1, not stay where it was at a ratio of 10. The engine holds it flat, and the commit block's step reaches only overpairs and better. **The mechanism this contract is missing is a continuous commitment slope over `TOP_PAIR` and `MIDDLE_PAIR` below `spr_commit`.** It is filed here as an open item for the single designated re-anchor slice; it is not authorized by this amendment, and it interacts with the existing commit block (§4 row P6), whose step it would partly subsume.

The ruling gives the highest-severity open engine defect a home and nothing more: **it still authorizes no build here.** **What the owner ruled is scope, and only scope** — the paragraph below, about how that work is packaged, is not part of the ruling.

### A9 — α is a per-RANGE bound; the 2026-08-19 per-bucket ruling is WITHDRAWN (owner-ratified 2026-08-24)

**Bottom line. The fold ceiling α binds a defender's WHOLE RANGE and nothing smaller. The 2026-08-19 ruling that applied it to one hand class — naked ace-high on the river — is withdrawn by the owner on 2026-08-24, and no test, ticket, band or review finding may assert a per-bucket α obligation again. One question survives the withdrawal and is recorded below as OPEN.**

Plain definitions, because the two words do the whole work here. A **bucket** is one hand-strength class the engine reasons in — `ACE_HIGH`, `MIDDLE_PAIR`, `OVERPAIR_TPTK` and the rest. A **range** is the seat's whole set of possible holdings at that decision. **α = f/(1+f)** is the minimum-defence identity: facing a bet of `f` times the pot, a defender who folds more than α of its range makes the bet profitable with any two cards. The quantity α bounds is a share **of the range** — it is a statement about how much total defence the range must supply, and it says nothing about which buckets supply it.

**Why the per-bucket reading is not merely unsourced but wrong, in both directions.** Applied to the ace-high bucket alone the identity is **too strict at large bets** — the range's stronger holdings already supply all the defence α demands, so ace-high may correctly fold outright — and **too loose at small bets**, where the same identity, read per-bucket, would demand ace-high continue essentially always. The arithmetic is in the slice-3 finding ledger, `docs/ai-dlc/ledger/flywheel-slice3-calldown.md`, item "Filed 10" (its numbered findings are the items that slice left for adjudication), and it is not close.

**What the withdrawal settles, so no later slice re-derives it.** The guard built to enforce the per-bucket reading failed for all six personas at all four prices — 24 of 24 cells, by +0.27 to +0.64 — and that is now **not a breach**, because there is no per-bucket obligation to breach (ledger Filed 9 closes with this). No re-derivation of `_ACE_HIGH_RIVER_CALL_DAMP` is owed against a bound that does not exist (ledger Filed 8 dissolves); the damp is a **lever**, independently pinned and documented in the engine, and this amendment does not touch it. The W3R-1 rule in §5a points exactly here rather than at a wider lever: per-bucket compliance would have required a constant roughly **60×** its shipped value, a magnitude the went-to-showdown bands already refused at 7.5×.

**What is unaffected.** §3's 42.9% correction and §9 ledger entry 1 quote α as a **price** — the fold ceiling for a given bet size — not as a per-bucket obligation, and both stand unchanged. §5a's W3R-1 rule stands unchanged; the "α wall" it names is the range-level wall.

**OPEN — the residual of ledger Filed 2, and it is the only thing this ruling leaves live.** May a **tight** archetype (the nit) sit closer to the range-level α wall than a loose one, or cross it deliberately, as real tight players do? **This stays OPEN.** It is a modelling-philosophy question with no source behind it today, and it becomes live only when a slice next pushes the nit's calling dial. **No test may admit it — as an assertion, a band edge or an acceptance criterion — until a sourced margin exists**, quoted with the provenance triple §5a's citing gate requires. A slice that wants it must open the target's provenance first, exactly as the W3R-1 rule directs.

**What this ruling costs, stated rather than implied.** The river loses its only ace-high-specific guard, and the one-way tripwire shipped with ticket S3-T4 (ticket 4 of improvement slice 3) goes with it. If ace-high folding is later judged a realism defect, that claim needs a **new, correctly grounded target**: the α identity may not be borrowed for it a second time.

**Scope: documentation and the removal of the tests that asserted the withdrawn obligation** (named in `docs/ai-dlc/specs/slice3-decisions-execution.md`). **No engine constant, lever, band or content-pack value changes with this amendment**, and it authorizes no build.

## 4a. Ratified amendments to the P3 and P6 rows (A1, A2, A3 — owner-ratified 2026-08-21)

**This subsection is the governing text for the commit brake's mechanism and scope, and for the one exception to P6's made-hand commitment rule.** The two §4 table cells above point here. The owner ratified amendments A1, A2 and A3 on 2026-08-21; the wording below is the ratified wording, with the one clause the draft deliberately left open — the board class in A3 — filled by the owner's ruling on question Q8, also 2026-08-21. **Ratification settles the definitions. Three internal labels are used below, in plain words. **P3** is this contract's row for the commit brake: the mechanism that raises a bot's willingness to fold as the chips it must call grow relative to its own stack. **P6** is this contract's row for the draw-jam fold, which also carries the sentence preserving made-hand commitment. **M6** is the matching mechanism on the betting-and-raising side: the same commitment fraction damping an aggressive bet the bot chooses to make, rather than a call it is facing.

### A1 — the commit brake's mechanism definition

*Replaces the "EXACT gate / boundary" cell of §4 row P3. Ratified by the owner, 2026-08-21.*

**P3 commit brake — mechanism.** The brake is a multiplicative term on the **fold** merit of a facing decision, keyed on the commitment fraction `c = to_call / stack`, where `to_call` is the amount the seat must put in to continue and `stack` is the seat's own remaining stack before that call. It **raises** fold merit as `c` grows and is a **no-op** at or below a dead-zone `c0` in the range 0.25 to 0.35, so that shallow-cost decisions stay byte-identical. It is a direction, never an asserted floor: the brake states that fold merit must be larger at higher `c`, and it never pins fold merit to a value.

**Denominator.** Any pot term the brake reads is the **pot before the current aggression**, the single definition §7 already unifies for the faced-fraction fix and the semi-bluff thresholds. The live pot variable already contains the bet being faced and silently lowers every threshold that reads it. The existing stack-to-pot commit gate reads the live pot today (`backend/app/domain/personas_postflop.py:1394`), so the slice that builds the brake **re-fits the existing per-persona `spr_commit` values in the same change**; those values were fitted against the forbidden denominator and do not survive the correction unchanged.

**Position in the stacked-multiplier order (§7).** The brake is **not** a member of the §7 aggressive-candidate chain and does not join its five-factor order. It applies to the fold entry only, after every aggressive-side multiplier has been applied and **before** the normalization the softmax law describes in §2. Its one ordering obligation is against the stack-to-pot commit gate that precedes it in the same function: that gate **zeroes** fold merit for a made hand, and a multiplicative brake applied after a zero is inert. The brake is therefore evaluated **only where the gate has not fired**, or, in the single spot A3 names, after the gate's zero has been replaced by a non-zero fold merit. A slice that ships the brake without stating which of those two paths it took has not defined its own mechanism.

**What the brake must not touch.** It must not scale any aggressive candidate (that is A2's boost side, and it is a separate lever with a separate boundary); it must not scale the draw call bonus (§9 ledger item 2 records that the fold-side brake alone does not fix that defect and that the bonus needs its own equity gate); it must not alter made-hand value-jam behaviour outside the exception A3 names; it must not add a random draw, and in particular must not add one before the action draw, which the villain-range estimator replays. `test_clamp_and_jam_edge` is the named non-byte-identical exception and the only one.

### A2 — P3's scope, as two named boundaries

*Replaces the words "Scope to **facing-fold merit ONLY**" in §4 row P3. Depends on A1. Ratified by the owner, 2026-08-21.*

**P3 scope — two boundaries, one wave.** The commit brake has a fold-side boundary and a boost-side boundary, and §7's joint-calibration rule requires both to ship in the same wave because they act on the same decision at the same `c`.

**Fold side (P3 proper).** As A1 defines it: raise fold merit on a facing decision as `c` grows, outside the dead zone.

**Boost side (M6).** The same `c` **damps** an *initiating* aggressive candidate — a bet or a raise the seat is choosing to make — when all of the following hold: the made-hand bucket is `AIR` or `ACE_HIGH`, the draw category is `NONE`, and the candidate's own size would commit at least the dead-zone fraction `c0` of the seat's stack. This is the narrow class the roadmap's canonical evidence hand exhibits: a hand with no pair and no draw raising most of a stack out of position into two opponents. The damp is a direction, not a prohibition; the bot must retain some frequency of large bluff-raises, because removing it entirely re-introduces a determinism of exactly the kind the improvement phase's first slice spent seven pull requests removing.

**Joint calibration.** The two sides are calibrated against the *combined* observed statistic, never one at a time, per §7. A slice that fits the fold side, measures, and then fits the boost side against the moved population has fitted a compensating error.

**What this does not license.** The boost-side damp may not touch made hands of any rung, may not touch semi-bluffs (any hand carrying a draw), and may not change bet **sizing**, which is governed by the anti-sizing-tell no-go in §8.

### A3 — the P6 made-hand fold exception, named

*Amends the sentence "Made hands (rung ≥ OVERPAIR_TPTK) keep low-SPR commit unchanged" in §4 row P6. Ratified by the owner, 2026-08-21, with the board-class clause settled by the Q8 ruling recorded below.*

**P6 made-hand commitment — one named exception.** Made hands keep their low-stack-to-pot commitment unchanged, with exactly one exception, and the exception is written as a spot rather than as a rung:

- the made-hand bucket is **`OVERPAIR_TPTK`** and no better — a set, two pair or any stronger holding is **excluded**, and stacking a set off at a stack-to-pot ratio near one remains correct play that the brake must preserve;
- the **board shows three cards to a straight (three board ranks inside a five-rank window) or three cards to a flush** — that is, any board on which some two-card holding already completes a straight or a flush. This is the clause the draft left as a placeholder for the owner; it was filled on 2026-08-21 with Candidate B of the two the draft stated (see the Q8 ruling below);
- the street is **turn or river**, and the seat is **facing a raise it did not initiate**, including but not limited to a check-raise;
- the commitment fraction `c` is at or above the dead zone `c0`.

In that spot, and nowhere else, the fold merit of a made hand may become non-zero. Everywhere else the commit gate's zero stands.

**The first gate on this exception is directional and zero-variance, not numeric.** Capture the normalized merit vector at a named spot and assert that fold probability is non-zero and strictly above its mechanic-off baseline. No numeric fold-frequency target may be written into a test for this exception until a source for that level exists, and a uniform fold target across archetypes is specifically refused: it would force a tight regular to over-fold against a maniac, which is the wrong adjustment for that matchup. Any test of this exception states the raise size, the opponent count, and the opponent's persona, because the correct answer differs across all three.

### The Q8 ruling — which board class the P6 exception fires on (owner, 2026-08-21)

**The owner chose Candidate B: three cards to a straight (three board ranks inside a five-rank window) or three to a flush — any board where some two-card holding already completes a straight or a flush.** Candidate A, the literal reading of the roadmap blocker, required **four** cards to a straight or a flush and was rejected.

**Why the question had to be answered rather than assumed.** The roadmap blocker names one predicate — an "overpair on a four-straight or four-flush board facing a check-raise" — and nineteen lines later mandates the falsification test that decides whether the mechanic works at all: a pair of aces on `Td 8s 2h │ Jc`, facing an 88-percent-pot turn raise, 85 big blinds behind, against two opponents. That board is four different suits and its four ranks span nine, so it is neither four to a flush nor four to a straight; the engine's own texture classifier returns `dry | rainbow | disconnected | unpaired` on it. Under the literal predicate the exception cannot fire on the board the slice is required to test it with, and the first evidence gate of the brake's build fails by construction.

**The three reasons the ruling rests on, as the draft stated them.** First, the pre-registered falsification test is the only gate anyone has written that can tell whether the mechanism works at all, and a predicate that cannot fire on it converts the gate into paperwork. Second, the poker supports it: an overpair facing a large turn raise on `J T 8` is a textbook fold candidate, and requiring a fourth card before the bot may even consider folding is tighter than the game is. Third, the breadth risk is containable by the levers A3 already specifies — the exception still requires a raise the seat did not initiate, on turn or river, at or above the dead-zone commitment fraction, and its first gate is directional rather than numeric, so "fires often" does not mean "folds often". The fold merit is to be kept deliberately small and the other three conditions of A3 are unchanged.

**Consequence for the build, stated so no later slice has to re-derive it.** The pre-registered falsification board `Td 8s 2h │ Jc` **stays valid and is not re-specified**: `J T 8` sits inside a five-rank window, so a villain holding `Q9` or `97` already has a straight and the exception fires. This predicate does fire on a large share of coordinated boards, so the exception is a common event rather than a rare one, and it needs a new small helper — the engine's existing classifier computes the span of the **whole** board and calls the mandated board disconnected, so it cannot be reused for this.

### Build authorization — the commit brake is DEFERRED past the phase-3 finale

**A1, A2 and A3 are ratified as definitions only. No build is authorized by this subsection.** The owner ruled on 2026-08-21 that the brake's build — the slice these three amendments unblock — is **deferred past the bot-realism flywheel's phase-3 finale and filed as a named post-finale slice**. The reasoning is the one the draft recommended: the brake slice is rated the highest regression risk on its own roadmap and needs a bracketing search rather than a gradient fit, because the stack-to-pot commit gate it composes with is a step function that swings one measured persona's raise frequency 28 points across a five-big-blind stack change, and that is a large thing to insert into a two-to-three-week phase with a single finale run at the end.

**The deferral carries a trigger, so the decision does not have to be re-argued from scratch.** If the calldown work — the continuation-frequency tuning on the calling dials that owns the bulk of the went-to-showdown movement — stalls with any persona still clearly outside its grounded band, **and** the residual is attributable to commit-gated nodes, then the scope amendment that admits the brake into the improvement phase is taken **at that moment**. The reason the trigger exists rather than a preference: dial work cannot reach a node where the commit gate has already zeroed fold merit, and that is precisely the class of pot the brake exists to make foldable, so the calldown work has a reach limit that nobody has measured yet.

#### A4.2 — the interim regime: how a mid-transition slice is judged

**From ratification until the single re-anchor closes, the six went-to-showdown legs are governed by a transition regime rather than by their frozen intervals. The regime is asymmetric on purpose: showdown frequency is meant to fall, so downward movement is legal and upward movement is not.**

1. **Floors are replaced by the grounded floor.** Each persona's lower bound becomes the low edge of its §5 target — nit 0.20, tag 0.25, lag 0.26, maniac 0.30, station 0.38, fish 0.33. A slice that lowers showdown frequency toward the target can no longer fail for doing so. 2. **Ceilings are kept and ratcheted downward.** After any slice that moves a persona's showdown frequency down, the ceiling is re-recorded at the new measurement plus three binomial standard deviations at the harness's own sample size, rounded outward to the nearest hundredth — **and never above the incumbent ceiling**. The ratchet is one-way. Recompute the arithmetic at whatever tip the ticket runs on rather than copying these numbers.
3. **Upward movement is refused by default and is escapable only on the record.** A slice whose change raises a persona's showdown frequency past its ratcheted ceiling does not widen the ceiling. It either lowers the strength of its change until it fits, as ticket T3 did, or it files an attribution note stating which street and which node class the increase comes from, why that class is not the one the re-anchor owns, and what the offsetting decrease will be — and the owner ratifies that note before the ticket merges. **The infeasibility rule in §5a still applies**: a slice that cannot reach a target stops and re-opens the target's provenance; it does not widen the lever, widen the band, or re-scope the test.

### The c-bet band verdict (W5-a2's required ruling)

**Verdict: do NOT move the c-bet band in this slice — and the roadmap's stated reason for expecting it to move is not the reason it may eventually need to.**

1. **The table-size hypothesis is unsupported by the only source that could test it.** The expectation was that 9-max's higher multiway incidence pushes the correct c-bet number **down**. The *mechanism* holds — multiway c-bet frequency is clearly and substantially lower than heads-up (S3 and several strategy sources), full ring does produce more multiway pots, and a second, independent mechanism points the same way (6-max post-flop ranges are weaker, so the correct 6-max adjustment is to barrel *more*). But **no source quantifies multiway incidence by table size**, and the one source that publishes flop c-bet for both formats side by side (S1) reports **no shift at all** (70/70). So the direction is plausible-to-likely; the magnitude is unmeasured; and the single format-comparative datum points at zero. That is not enough to move a band.
2. **The row has a larger defect than table size: a class mismatch.** §5's band (TAG 55–70, LAG 60–75, maniac 80–95) is shaped like a **heads-up-flop reg target**. The harness aggregate it is judged against is shaped like an **all-spots population average**, where the observed range is 40–60 (S3, S5). A −20pp "miss" is exactly the size of that class gap. **The measured gap is partly, and possibly wholly, an artifact of comparing two different statistics.**
3. **The instrument is independently known to be broken, and W5-a3-i owns it.** The roadmap already records that metric #1 computes P(bet | first-in flop decision) for *any* tested seat — including cold-callers and blind defenders who check most flops — not aggressor-side c-bet, and that it **under-reads every persona** (tag 0.417 vs 0.488, nit 0.224 vs 0.326, fish 0.196 vs 0.321). The maniac −20pp at n=415 is a reading from that instrument. **A band may not be re-anchored against an instrument its own roadmap has already declared mis-specified** — doing so is the band-laundering §11 item 7 exists to catch.
4. **So the §5a obligation-(2) / W3R-1 test comes out the other way here.** The rule says an unreachable target is evidence about the *target*. It presumes the *measurement* is sound. Here it is not, and two independent confounders (metric denominator, HU-vs-aggregate scope) sit between the bots and the band. **Re-open the target only after both are cleared.**

**Ruling, in order:** (a) c-bet band **unchanged**, `[UNVERIFIED]` on level, DIRECTIONAL-only, **never a HARD gate** while unverified — which also means **W5-a3-i's re-read must not be used to re-anchor it**; (b) sequence **W5-a3-i** (aggressor-side denominator) first, then decide whether §5's row is a heads-up target or an aggregate one and **say so in the row label** — the two cannot share a number; (c) only then, with a population-class 9-max source in hand, re-open the level. **Expected direction if it does move: DOWN, conf MEDIUM. Expected magnitude: unknown — deliberately not guessed.**

### The two obligations

**(1) The citing gate.** A ticket, spec, or slice that cites a §5 target MUST quote its provenance triple. **A bare number FAILs review.** Citing an `[UNVERIFIED]` row is allowed only as DIRECTIONAL evidence — such a row may **never** be made a HARD gate while unverified. Under the rule immediately above, that provenance class does not qualify for a HARD gate.

**Why the owner made the exception anyway, and what a reviewer must therefore not do.** The 43–53% figure is the only band in the roster that has been independently re-validated against live population data — the 2026-08-05 re-measure read live 50.0 against sim 52.0 over 1,228 live hands and 50,000 simulated hands, and reported it IN band — and the same test's own docstring already records the standing rule that *"the band is the gate and must not be widened"* for this statistic. The owner judged a live-data-corroborated envelope worth gating on despite its soft label. Consequences a reviewer must respect: **(a)** this is a **one-off, named** exception and it does **not** reopen the grandfather clause — no other slice may add a HARD gate on a DIRECTIONAL row by citing it; **(b)** the correct response to this gate going red is **not** to widen it, because widening it is what the standing rule forbids and it would trade away the one thing this band has in its favour; **(c)** the margin has changed, and a red must be read against the CURRENT one. **Before** the 2026-08-19 re-tune that shipped as pull request #205, the lag cleared the 0.43 floor by only 0.72 percentage points — about 0.96 standard errors, with the test's own printed Wilson interval already dipping under the floor — while three postflop-only commits had been observed to move the same reading by about 0.43 percentage points through shared-random-number-stream displacement alone. (Figures verified against the docstring of `test_n3bstrata_production_opener_blend_in_dossier_band` in `backend/tests/test_personas_postflop.py`, which records both states.)

**(2) The W3R-1 rule — infeasibility is evidence about the TARGET.** When a fit cannot reach a target using a legitimate range or lever, the slice **STOPS and re-opens that target's provenance**. It does **not** widen the lever, widen the band, or re-scope the test to dodge the number. Three separate slices hit the α wall and each escaped by node-scoping instead of resolving it; that pattern is what this rule exists to stop.

The transfer caveat is therefore now **narrowed, not lifted** — it stands in full for the c-bet level and for W$SD, and it stands as a `LOW`-confidence "probably fine" everywhere else postflop.

**Standing rule:** any *other* magnitude in this contract that traces to a 6-max solver output and is quoted at 9-max is suspect by default and must be re-checked against a full-ring source before it is written into a test as a gate. Recreational-archetype anchors are the exception — they are table-size-insensitive, so the transfer is harmless there (§5).

**Standing rule, second limb (added by W5-a2, ledger #15):** a **format-comparative** source — one that publishes both formats side by side and can therefore be asked the difference — outranks a better-known source that reports a single pooled number, *for the format question only*. For the **level**, the ranking inverts: prefer a population-class measurement over an ideal-target one, and **match the class of the source to the class of the harness metric** before comparing them at all. Most of the −20pp c-bet "miss" is a class mismatch, not a behavior deficit.

## 11. Reviewer checklist (apply to a slice's output — each item is pass/fail)

The most important section. For a persona-realism slice under review:

*(Note added 2026-08-24 with item 16.* **Two items here are not only retrospective.** *Item 15 carries the W3R-1 rule and item 16 carries the reduction-floor registration rule; both are obligations that bind a slice while it is being built, and each is written as a binding rule plus a pass/fail check. A slice does not satisfy them by being asked about them at review.)*

1. **[Softmax law]** Are ALL new magnitudes justified by a MEASURED (observed closed-loop) stat hitting its target, or are any dropped-in constants closing the slice on "the constant is in the code"? Any un-fit constant → **FAIL**.
2. **[Metric-DoD]** If the slice needs a NEW harness metric to prove its effect, is that metric live AND showing the expected direction? If not, the slice cannot close on a HARD gate → **FAIL** (unless correctly self-labeled DIRECTIONAL).
3. **[Gate boundary — §4]** Does the mechanic's gate exactly match §4? Specifically: P2 damp gates MIDDLE_PAIR/TOP_PAIR only (NOT OVERPAIR_TPTK)? P5 floor is MIDDLE_PAIR only? P4 street mult on the bluff side only (made `_AGG_BASE` does NOT decline)? **P3 commit brake — does it honor amendment A2's two named boundaries: the fold side (raises fold merit on a facing decision as the committed fraction `c` grows, per A1) and the boost side (damps an initiating bet/raise only for AIR or ACE_HIGH with no draw, sized to commit at least the dead-zone fraction, per A2's conditions) — shipped in ONE wave, calibrated jointly against the combined observed statistic rather than fitted one side at a time?** *(Amended 2026-08-21, owner ruling folded into the slice-3 calldown spec approval: this item previously read "P3 commit brake scoped to facing-fold merit only?", which A2 already superseded in §4 on 2026-08-19 but §11 never picked up — the old wording would fail a correctly built brake. Any → **FAIL**.

Demanding a hard match on a directional target FAILS good work → **FAIL the criterion**. Conversely, are the three HARD-today gates (AF, Fold-to-C-bet, WTSD) actually checked where applicable?
7. **[Band re-anchor — §7]** Was any band re-anchored **mid-spine**? The only legitimate band re-anchor is the SINGLE Wave-4 cluster re-measure, and there are now **two** named exceptions rather than one: the existing P5 unit-assertion split, and **a band move made under the transition regime in §5's A4.2, which is legal only in the ratcheting direction and only accompanied by the measurement, the sample size, and the standard-deviation arithmetic that produced it.** A ceiling that moves up, a floor that moves down below its grounded edge, or a move with no arithmetic beside it still **FAILS**. Any other mid-spine band widening → **FAIL**. (Second exception added 2026-08-21 by owner-ratified amendment A7, which exists so that A4's interim regime is reviewable rather than auto-failed by this item.)

Read literally, the sentence above would fail the very change that installed the regime, because A5 moves a ceiling up. It is spent: it was taken once, in the change that installed the regime, and it licenses no further upward move by any persona for any reason. Every other ceiling move remains downward-only, and a slice citing A5 as precedent **FAILS**. ⚠️ **Provenance of this clause: it is an orchestrator-adjudicated clarification of how two ratified blocks (A5 and A7) interact, dated 2026-08-21, and is flagged for owner confirmation. It is not itself owner-ratified text.**
8. **[Intentional-leave — §8]** Did the slice "fix" F12 (aggression-cap compression) or F14 (no strength-correlated sizing)? Either → **FAIL**.
9. **[Estimator parity — §7]** Does the slice make the LIVE bot diverge from the streetless policy? If so, did it thread `range_estimate.py` the SAME context and add a parity test? Divergence without parity → **FAIL**.
10. **[Default-off byte-identity]** Do new args default to today's behavior so `range_estimate` + the population harness stay byte-identical for un-opted-in callers? Is the action draw still the FIRST `rng.choices` (new randomness only after)? If not → **FAIL**.
11. **[Denominator unification — §7]** If the slice touches faced_frac / commit-gate / T1, does it use ONE "pot before current aggression" denominator (not live `pot_bb`)? If not → **FAIL**.
12. **[Stacked-multiplier order — §7]** If the slice adds a multiplier, is it applied to the whole aggressive candidate in the §10.2 order, and is the *combined* product calibrated (not each factor independently)? If not → **FAIL**.
13. **[Domain purity + scope]** Does the slice keep `personas*.py` pure (no web/DB), leave the grader / `spot_signature()` untouched, and stay inside the files its ticket names? Any breach → **FAIL**.
14. **[Coverage delta]** Did the slice report the cumulative graded-coverage delta vs the immutable snapshot, and is any loss adjudicated? Silent loss → **FAIL**.
15. **[Target provenance — §5a]** Does the slice cite a §5 target as a **bare number**, with no `(format, pool/stakes, source)` triple? → **FAIL**. Does it transfer a format-SENSITIVE stat across table sizes without restating it, or gate HARD on an `[UNVERIFIED]` row? → **FAIL**. And the W3R-1 rule: did the slice fail to reach a target and respond by widening a lever, widening a band, or re-scoping the test — instead of stopping and re-opening that target's provenance? → **FAIL**. **This contract is not immune:** a format/pool mismatch found here is a **CONTRACT-DEFECT at HIGH**, and the slice may not pass on the contract's authority alone.
16. **[Reduction-floor registration — owner-ratified 2026-08-24]** A "reduction floor" is the pre-registered minimum movement a change must produce to count as having worked — for example, "this retune must cut the statistic by at least so many points." It is written down **before** the measurement, which is the whole point of it.
    - **The binding rule (prospective — it binds BEFORE the slice ships, not only when a reviewer reaches this line):** a reduction floor is registered **against the configuration proposed to ship**. **If the ship list changes — a persona dropped or added at a go/no-go gate, a lever pulled from the change — the floors are RE-DERIVED before the values land.** A floor registered against a configuration that is not the one shipped is not evidence about the change: it manufactures a shortfall that is real arithmetic against a bot nobody built. Registering a floor against a superseded ship list, or shipping values under one, breaches this rule at the moment it happens; a reviewer catching it later is the backstop, not the enforcement.
    - **The reviewer check (pass/fail):** **was every reduction floor in this slice registered against the configuration the slice actually shipped, and re-derived if the ship list moved between registration and merge?** A floor carried over from a superseded ship list, or a shortfall reported against one, → **FAIL**.
    - *(Provenance and why it is written in two halves. Ticket S3-T5 — ticket 5 of improvement slice 3 — registered a reduction floor against a three-persona configuration and then shipped one persona, producing a shortfall that was real against the registered floor and misleading about the change. The rule was proposed as item "Filed 15" of the slice-3 finding ledger and adopted by the owner on 2026-08-24 as slice-3 close decision 4. It is written in the **same dual form as the W3R-1 rule** — a binding obligation stated in §5a's "two obligations" and separately enforced as a pass/fail question in item 15 above — because both reviewers of the slice-3 decisions spec made the same point: landing a prospective rule as a bare checklist item silently demotes it into an after-the-fact question. **Gain:** it prevents a recurring class of fake misses on any gate-decided ship list. **Cost:** one contract edit, and genuinely more re-derivation work on tickets whose ship list is decided late — which is the work the rule exists to force. Recorded in §9 entry 18.)*
